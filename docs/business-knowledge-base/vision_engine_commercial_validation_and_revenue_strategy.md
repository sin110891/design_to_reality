## 1. What you now need to prove

The Vision Engine evaluation proves **design quality**. The next stage must prove **commercial utility**.

You need evidence that the product:

1. saves professional time  
2. reduces unpaid pre-sales work  
3. improves client conversion  
4. reduces revision cycles  
5. produces outputs that can become a real project scope  
6. becomes more valuable as customer data accumulates  

The critical shift is:

> From “our AI produces better concepts”  
> to “our system helps firms win and deliver more profitable projects.”

# 2. Who are the perfect first customers?

The best initial customer is not simply “an architecture firm.”

Your ideal customer should have:

- repeated residential or light-commercial projects
- visually led client sales
- high amounts of unpaid concept work
- project values high enough to justify software
- relatively standardised room types
- decision-makers accessible to the founders

Typical characteristics:

- 5–30 employees
- projects worth £30,000–£300,000
- kitchens, living spaces, bathrooms and full refurbishments
- internal designer or architect

This is probably the strongest category because it combines:

The Vision Engine can eventually affect the entire revenue journey.

# 3. What can you save them?

These numbers must be validated during pilots. They are initial estimates.

## Existing manual workflow

For one medium residential concept:

| Activity | Estimated time |
|---|---:|
| Interpret client brief | 1–2 hours |
| Search references | 2–4 hours |
| Build mood board | 2–5 hours |
| Produce early visual concept | 4–12 hours |
| Materials research | 2–6 hours |
| Revisions | 3–10 hours |
| Initial scope preparation | 2–6 hours |
| **Total** | **16–45 hours** |

At an internal blended cost of approximately **£35–£75 per hour**, this represents:

> **£560–£3,375 of staff cost per concept**

Some of this work is charged to the customer. Much of it may be absorbed as pre-sales work.

## Vision Engine target

A credible pilot target would be:

- concept preparation reduced from 16–45 hours to 4–12 hours
- reference search reduced by 60–90%
- first visual direction produced within 30–90 minutes
- revision rounds reduced from 3–5 to 1–3
- initial materials and trade scope created automatically

### Potential saving per project

Estimated:

- **8–25 hours saved**
- approximately **£300–£1,800 internal cost saved**
- potentially faster client approval by several days
- additional project capacity without immediately hiring

For a firm producing 10 concepts per month:

- 80–250 hours saved monthly
- approximately £3,000–£18,000 potential monthly labour capacity

The real value may be larger if the tool helps win one additional £50,000–£150,000 renovation project.

# 4. How to prove customers will pay

Do not ask:

> “Would you use this?”

Ask them to pay for a defined commercial outcome.

## Paid pilot structure

Run a **6–8 week paid pilot**.

### Pilot pricing

Early pilots:

- £500–£1,500 per firm
- or £250–£500 per live project
- refunded or credited against subscription only if the system fails agreed criteria

Avoid free pilots unless the customer provides exceptional data, access or distribution.

## Pilot conditions

Each company should use the system on:

- 3–10 real client opportunities
- at least one project they have not yet won
- at least one existing project requiring revisions
- the same staff who perform the current manual workflow

## Measure before and after

For every project capture:

- hours to first concept
- staff role and cost
- number of concepts created
- number of revision rounds
- time to client approval
- whether the project converted
- value of project
- time to initial scope
- time to quotation
- percentage of output retained
- manual corrections required
- software cost per accepted concept

## Payment proof ladder

The strongest evidence progresses as follows:

1. customer agrees the problem exists  
2. customer shares current workflow data  
3. customer tests with real projects  
4. customer pays for pilot  
5. customer renews  
6. customer expands seats or usage  
7. customer attributes revenue or savings to the system  

A paid pilot is useful. Renewal is much stronger.

# 6. Proving each unresolved question

## “Customers will pay”

Evidence required:

- 5–10 paid pilots
- at least 50% pilot-to-paid conversion
- minimum 3 customers renewing
- pricing resistance tested at multiple tiers
- at least one customer paying £500+ monthly

## “The product saves time and money”

Evidence required:

- timed baseline workflow
- timed product workflow
- actual employee cost
- project-level calculation
- customer-signed case study

Target:

- 40%+ reduction in concept time
- 30%+ reduction in revisions
- 25%+ reduction in time to scope
- payback within one or two projects

## “Outputs are commercially usable”

Define commercially usable precisely:

- client can understand and approve direction
- materials are plausible
- major geometry is preserved
- output can be translated into scope
- professional needs limited correction
- no serious buildability contradiction

Target:

- 70%+ of projects produce one acceptable concept within three attempts
- 60%+ of generated concept content retained
- fewer than 15% severe geometry failures

## “The advantage survives scale”

Test across:

- room types
- project values
- photography quality
- lighting conditions
- customer briefs
- styles
- reviewers

Do not immediately claim all design styles.

Start with:

> Contemporary European kitchens, living spaces and bathrooms.

Then expand dataset coverage deliberately.

## “Competitors cannot reproduce it”

You cannot prove they cannot reproduce the interface.

You can build advantages that become increasingly difficult to reproduce:

- proprietary reviewed design records
- accepted and rejected concepts
- client revision histories
- links between concept and actual materials
- links between concept and final quote
- links between concept and completed result
- architect reviewer reputation system
- customer-specific design memory
- workflow integrations

The moat is not image generation. It is the accumulated chain:

> brief → concept → expert correction → client choice → scope → quote → completed outcome

# 8. Which customer category should you prioritise?

Recommended order:

1. **premium design-and-build firms**
2. **kitchen and bathroom firms**
3. **premium renovation businesses**
4. **interior-design studios**
5. **small architecture practices**

Why:

- design-and-build connects design directly to revenue and delivery
- kitchen/bathroom firms have repeatable rooms and measurable sales conversion
- premium renovators have high project values
- pure design studios have value but weaker connection to downstream transactions
- architecture practices may adopt slowly and have lower budgets
